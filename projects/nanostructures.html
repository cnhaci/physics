Light–Matter Interactions in Nanostructures

A computational study of plasmonic interactions in silver nanoparticle systems, focusing on three-dimensional particle geometry, scattering spectra, extinction spectra, and resonance behaviour.

1. Introduction

Nanostructures provide a useful framework for studying the interaction between electromagnetic radiation and matter at the nanoscale.

In this project, numerical simulations were used to investigate the optical response of silver nanoparticle systems. The study focuses on the geometry of interacting nanoparticles and their wavelength-dependent scattering and extinction responses.

2. Project Overview

The project consists of three main computational results. First, a three-dimensional surface mesh representation of two silver nanoparticles was examined.

The scattering spectrum of the two-particle system was then analysed as a function of wavelength. Finally, the extinction spectrum of a single silver nanosphere with a diameter of 100 nm was investigated.

4. Scattering Spectrum

The scattering spectrum describes the wavelength-dependent scattering cross section of the two-silver-nanoparticle system. The spectrum shows a pronounced resonance associated with the plasmonic interaction between the nanoparticles.

Figure 2. Scattering spectrum of the two-silver-nanoparticle system. The main resonance occurs at approximately 560 nm.

5. Extinction Spectrum

The extinction spectrum represents the wavelength-dependent extinction cross section of a single silver nanosphere, including the combined contribution of absorption and scattering.

The simulated nanosphere has a diameter of 100 nm and exhibits a characteristic localized surface plasmon resonance (LSPR) response.

Figure 3. Extinction spectrum of a single silver nanosphere with a diameter of 100 nm.

6. Discussion & Conclusion

The simulations demonstrate the optical response of silver nanoparticle systems through their scattering and extinction spectra.

The two-particle scattering spectrum shows a pronounced resonance at approximately 560 nm, highlighting the wavelength-dependent response of the interacting nanoparticle system.

The extinction spectrum of the 100 nm silver nanosphere provides the corresponding optical response of a single nanoparticle and reveals its characteristic LSPR behaviour.

Overall, the project provides a computational representation of nanoparticle geometry and demonstrates how numerical simulations can be used to investigate light–matter interactions at the nanoscale.
