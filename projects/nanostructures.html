Light–Matter Interactions in Nanostructures

A computational study of plasmonic interactions in silver nanoparticle systems, focusing on nanoparticle geometry, scattering spectra, extinction spectra, and resonance behaviour.

1. Introduction

Nanostructures provide a framework for investigating the interaction between electromagnetic radiation and matter at the nanoscale.

In this project, numerical simulations were used to study the optical response of silver nanoparticle systems. The analysis focuses on nanoparticle geometry, scattering behaviour, extinction, and plasmonic resonance.

2. Project Overview

The project examines two different nanoparticle configurations. First, a three-dimensional surface mesh representation of two silver nanoparticles separated by a defined gap is presented.

The optical response of the two-particle system is then investigated through its wavelength-dependent scattering spectrum. Finally, the extinction spectrum of a single silver nanosphere with a diameter of 100 nm is examined.

4. Scattering Spectrum

The scattering spectrum describes the wavelength-dependent scattering cross section of the two-silver-nanoparticle system.

The spectrum shows a pronounced resonance associated with the plasmonic interaction between the two nanoparticles. The main resonance occurs at approximately 560 nm.

Figure 2. Wavelength-dependent scattering cross section of the two-silver-nanoparticle system. The main resonance occurs at approximately 560 nm.

5. Extinction Spectrum

The extinction spectrum represents the wavelength-dependent extinction cross section of a single silver nanosphere.

Extinction includes the combined contribution of absorption and scattering. The simulated nanosphere has a diameter of 100 nm and exhibits a characteristic localized surface plasmon resonance (LSPR) response.

Figure 3. Extinction spectrum of a single silver nanosphere with a diameter of 100 nm.

6. Discussion & Conclusion

The simulations demonstrate the optical response of silver nanoparticle systems through their scattering and extinction spectra.

The scattering spectrum of the two-particle system exhibits a pronounced resonance at approximately 560 nm, demonstrating the wavelength-dependent optical response associated with plasmonic interaction.

The extinction spectrum of the 100 nm silver nanosphere provides the optical response of a single nanoparticle and shows its characteristic LSPR behaviour.

Overall, the project demonstrates how numerical simulations can be used to investigate nanoparticle geometry and light–matter interactions at the nanoscale.
