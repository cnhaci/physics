Project 02

Numerical Analysis of Weather Data

Analysis of real-world NOAA weather data using Python, numerical methods, statistical analysis, and data visualization.

Python Pandas NumPy Matplotlib Seaborn Interpolation Data Analysis

Overview

This project was developed for the Numerical Analysis course at Hacettepe University. The study focuses on the analysis of real-world hourly weather observations collected from the NOAA Local Climatological Data (LCD) dataset for the year 2024.

Two geographically and climatically different locations were selected: Honolulu, Hawaii, and Anchorage, Alaska. The main objective was to apply numerical and data analysis techniques to investigate weather patterns, relationships between variables, and missing data.

Dataset

Weather Stations

  • Honolulu International Airport, Hawaii
  • Ted Stevens Anchorage International Airport, Alaska

Analyzed Variables

  • Hourly Dry Bulb Temperature
  • Relative Humidity
  • Wind Speed
  • Precipitation
  • Sea Level Pressure

Methodology

1. Data Preprocessing

The raw NOAA datasets were loaded and processed using Pandas. Relevant meteorological variables were selected and numerical columns were converted into appropriate numerical formats.

2. Time-Based Analysis

The DATE column was converted into datetime format. Additional variables such as month, hour, and station were created to enable time-based and station-based comparisons.

3. Missing Data and Interpolation

Missing numerical observations were handled using linear interpolation. The method estimates missing values from neighboring observations in the time-ordered dataset.

data["Temperature"] = data["Temperature"].interpolate(method="linear")

4. Data Visualization

Several visualization techniques were used to investigate the structure of the dataset:

  • Monthly average temperature plots
  • Temperature boxplots
  • Temperature histograms
  • Humidity-temperature scatter plots
  • Correlation heatmaps

Data Visualization

Several visualization techniques were used to compare the climatic characteristics of Honolulu and Anchorage and to examine relationships between the selected weather variables.

Figure 1. Monthly average temperature comparison between Honolulu and Anchorage.

Monthly Temperature Trends

Honolulu remains warm throughout the year with relatively small seasonal changes. In contrast, Anchorage shows strong seasonal variation, with very cold winter temperatures and substantially warmer summer temperatures.

Figure 2. Temperature distribution comparison between the two stations.

Temperature Comparison

The boxplot shows that Honolulu has a relatively narrow temperature distribution centered around the upper 70s °F. Anchorage has a much wider distribution, ranging from temperatures below 0°F to above 70°F.

Figure 3. Temperature distribution of Honolulu and Anchorage observations.

Temperature Distribution

Honolulu temperatures are strongly concentrated around approximately 75–80°F. Anchorage has a much broader distribution, reflecting its stronger seasonal variability.

Figure 4. Relationship between relative humidity and temperature.

Humidity vs. Temperature

The scatter plot shows a negative relationship between relative humidity and temperature. Honolulu observations remain concentrated at relatively high temperatures, whereas Anchorage covers a much wider temperature range.

Figure 5. Correlation matrix of the selected weather variables.

Correlation Analysis

The correlation heatmap indicates a negative relationship between relative humidity and temperature, while humidity also shows a negative relationship with wind speed. Precipitation has relatively weak correlations with the other variables.

Honolulu showed relatively stable temperatures throughout the year, with most observations concentrated around the upper 70s °F.

Anchorage showed strong seasonal variation, with very cold winter temperatures and significantly warmer summer conditions.

The comparison demonstrated that temperature was one of the strongest factors distinguishing the two locations.

Interpolation Analysis

Linear interpolation was used to estimate missing temperature observations from neighboring measurements. The interpolated values closely followed the original temperature series, demonstrating that the method was effective for filling short gaps in the time series.

Figure 6. Original and interpolated temperature data.

Original vs. Interpolated Temperature

The graph compares the original temperature observations with the values obtained after interpolation.

Note: Linear interpolation is more appropriate for continuous variables such as temperature. Its application to precipitation should be interpreted with caution because precipitation can change abruptly.

Discussion & Conclusion

This project demonstrated how numerical analysis and computational tools can be applied to real-world scientific datasets. Python libraries such as Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, and Seaborn were used throughout the analysis.

The comparison between Honolulu and Anchorage revealed substantial climatic differences. Honolulu displayed stable warm conditions, whereas Anchorage exhibited much stronger seasonal variability.

The interpolation analysis showed that linear interpolation can effectively reconstruct missing values in continuous weather variables such as temperature. However, the method should be applied carefully to variables such as precipitation where abrupt changes are common.

Overall, the project combined data cleaning, numerical interpolation, statistical analysis, visualization, and scientific interpretation into a single workflow.
